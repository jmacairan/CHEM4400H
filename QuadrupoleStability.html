Quadrupole Stability Explorer

The rods carry a DC voltage U and an RF voltage of amplitude V. For each ion these two voltages collapse into two numbers, a and q. Those two numbers alone decide if the ion gets through. The result can be drawn two ways: one universal window in (a, q), or one window per m/z in (U, V). This page shows both.

1One ion: a, q and the stability window

Rod pairs sit at +(U − V cos Ωt) and −(U − V cos Ωt). Pick an m/z and set U and V. The dot shows where that ion lands on the stability diagram. Inside the green window its path stays bounded in both x and y. Outside, the path grows until it hits a rod.

Try this.
  1. Raise only V. The dot slides right. Past the right edge the ion is thrown into the x rods. This edge removes ions that are too light.
  2. Raise only U. The dot climbs. Above the left edge the ion drifts into the y rods. This edge removes ions that are too heavy.
  3. Change only m/z. The dot runs along the line. Only a short piece of the line is inside the window.
  4. Set U = 0. The line lies on the q axis. Every ion with q below 0.908 passes. The quadrupole is now an ion guide.
  5. Switch to the voltage view and change only m/z. Now the dot stays and the window moves. Same physics, different axes.

2From stability window to mass filter

A real scan keeps the ratio U/V fixed and ramps both voltages together. In the universal view the window stays put and the ions slide outward along the line, heaviest last. In the voltage view each m/z has its own window and the setting climbs the scan line through the tip of each one. Each ion reaches the detector only while it is inside its window. The ratio U/V sets how much of the tip is cut, so it sets the resolution.

Voltage view. One window per m/z, the setting moves.

The scan line is U = ratio × V. It cuts the tip of each window in turn. Drag the dot to change V and the ratio. Windows that hold the dot are drawn bold. The V axis lines up with the spectrum below.

Top: the seven ions in the beam on a true m/z axis. The green band is what the filter passes right now. Bottom: detector signal recorded as V is ramped. Colours match the ions. At low resolution each peak is wide and starts early, because the ion enters the window before it reaches the apex.

Try this.
  1. Run the scan and watch both plots. Left: one window, moving ions. Right: fixed windows, moving setting. They always agree.
  2. Press RF only and run the scan. There is no upper mass limit, so the signal is a staircase. Ions drop out from the light end as V rises.
  3. Step through low → medium → high. The line cuts closer to the apex on the left and closer to the tips on the right. The pass band narrows and the peaks sharpen.
  4. At 0.1670 the pairs 100/101, 200/202 and 400/404 all separate at the same setting. A line through the origin gives constant m/Δm, so peaks get wider at high mass.
  5. Press too high. The line misses the window on the left and passes above every tip on the right. Nothing reaches the detector at any V.
